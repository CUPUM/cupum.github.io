On November 6, 2026, Rashid Mushkani, who completed his PhD at the UNESCO Chair in Urban Landscape this year, presented two papers at the highlighted poster session of ACM EAAMO 2026, the Conference on Equity and Access in Algorithms, Mechanisms, and Optimization, held at LMU Munich. Both posters ask the same question from two angles: how can AI support public decisions about cities without leaving residents and accountability behind?

The first, "WeDesign+: Deliberative Visual AI for Public Space Planning," co-authored with Hugo Bérard and Shin Koseki, studies how residents and planning professionals in Montréal use AI-generated images to explore and discuss public spaces. It follows how participants' ideas change through prompting, image revisions and group discussion, and how their original input can be preserved along the way. The second, "Decision-Time Administrative Reason-Giving for U.S. Municipal AI: The Urban Reasonableness Layer," proposes a framework for cities to document and justify AI-supported decisions before approving them, checking legal authority, evidence, alternatives and impacts. Using a housing-inspection simulation, it shows that explicit allocation constraints can reduce disproportionate inspection burdens with only a small reduction in hazard coverage.

Congratulations to Rashid on these presentations, which carry the Chair's work on participatory and accountable urban AI to an international audience. The WeDesign+ paper is available now, and the second paper will follow soon.
