Looking back on last spring: on 13 May 2026, Chairholder Shin Koseki spoke at the Université de Genève during "Gouverner la double transition des territoires: jumeaux numériques, complexité, participation" (Governing the twin transition of territories: digital twins, complexity, participation), the closing colloquium of the G3 project on the twin transition. Organized by Nicolas Baya-Laffite at Uni Mail, the event brought together lectures by Nicolas Baya-Laffite, Giovanna Di Marzo Serugendo, Shin Koseki and D. Vorbach, as well as a survey by students of the Master SACHA. Building on the work carried out since 2024 on the ecological and digital transitions, the colloquium opened a new research agenda on participatory digital twins, bringing together STS, urban planning and computer science to question the promises, uses and limits of these technologies.

In his lecture, "Concevoir et gouverner les systèmes complexes" (Designing and governing complex systems), Shin Koseki started from Aristophanes, whose comedies already denounced political disengagement, social exclusion and the manipulation of public opinion in Athens. He argued that these same limits of democracy still prevent decision-makers from acting on today's most urgent challenges, from climate change to ecosystem collapse, with only four years left before the 2030 Agenda deadline. He presented the Chair's vision for transforming citizen participation, the contemporary form of the citizens' assembly, by combining digital twins and artificial intelligence in open, interoperable tools co-created with citizens, local organizations and public institutions.

We warmly thank Nicolas Baya-Laffite (Université de Genève) for organizing the colloquium, Wouter Achten (Université libre de Bruxelles) for three years of collaboration, and the G3, the partnership between the Université de Montréal, the Université de Genève and the Université libre de Bruxelles, for its support. This visit was part of a five-week mission made possible by the FRQ, the Canadian Commission for UNESCO and the G3 global partnership.
