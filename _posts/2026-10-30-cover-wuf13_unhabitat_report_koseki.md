UN-Habitat Canada has released its report on Canada's participation in the Thirteenth World Urban Forum (WUF13), held in Baku, Azerbaijan, from 17 to 22 May 2026 under the theme "Housing the World: Safe and Resilient Cities and Communities." The report features "AI-Enabled Participation for Inclusive and Resilient Cities," the keynote session delivered at the Canada Pavilion by Chairholder Shin Koseki on behalf of the UNESCO Chair in Urban Landscape.

Led by Housing, Infrastructure and Communities Canada (HICC), the Canada Pavilion served throughout the Forum as a meeting point for governments, Indigenous representatives, international partners, academia and civil society. As part of the pavilion's day on the climate–housing nexus, Shin Koseki's keynote examined how artificial intelligence can strengthen participatory approaches to urban planning: broadening public engagement, bringing a wider range of perspectives into decisions, integrating local knowledge and identifying urban vulnerabilities. He also stressed the governance frameworks needed to ensure transparency and accountability, mitigate algorithmic bias and build public trust.

We warmly thank Housing, Infrastructure and Communities Canada for organizing the Canada Pavilion and for welcoming the Chair, and UN-Habitat Canada, based in Montréal, for preparing this report and for its continued partnership.
